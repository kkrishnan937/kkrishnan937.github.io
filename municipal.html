Clarify the process before choosing AI

The scenario examines missing enquiry details, repeated responsibility checks and uncertain handoffs. I explored when staff retain responsibility, what confirms acceptance and who can approve guidance or settle departmental boundaries.

The assessment compared routing assistance, FAQ assistance, trend analysis and guidance/intake improvement. Departmental routing depended on ownership rules that were not established across scope.

Choose a task the evidence can support

The smaller experiment classified the resident's reported topic: missed collection, reported dumped waste or Other. Unclear and mixed reports required human review. Missing addresses or dates were handled separately from the type.

Classification does not decide departmental ownership.

This boundary allowed a limited test without pretending the unresolved service responsibilities were agreed.

The process maps, stakeholder decision rights, options comparison, baseline-measure definitions and data-readiness assessment keep that distinction visible. Operational baselines were proposed, not measured.

Twenty development examples informed one prompt revision. The guide, rules, prompt and settings were then frozen. Forty evaluation candidates were reviewed before predictions; two overlap cases were excluded, leaving thirty-eight.

The local model improved clear-case classification but handled fewer review-required cases correctly. The predefined comparison rule required improvement without worse review handling.

Recommendation: do not deploy either tested method.

The model failed the safeguard. The simple baseline also performed poorly on unfamiliar wording. Higher headline counts did not establish operational value.

A separate local tracking prototype demonstrated recording, responsibility, cover, access controls and history. It supports the requirements work; it is not the classifier and does not verify a municipal system.

Limits of the conclusion

  • Enquiries and stakeholder conversations were simulated. Synthetic and related scenarios do not represent municipal demand.
  • Labels lack independent service-owner validation. The author and reviewer shared context; scenario overlap remains after two exclusions.
  • One model, one final run and a simple baseline do not establish general performance. The baseline is not a mature rules system.
  • No operational savings or service improvement was measured. Model runtime is not staff effort, and review workload remains unmeasured.
  • The local prototype does not establish production security, full manual sign-off or municipal readiness. Operational guidance publication remains on hold; no Azure Pipeline was run.
